\begin{abstract}
At Venus, absolute position is normally recovered through radiometric tracking by the Deep Space Network (DSN). When a balloon drifts onto the far side of the planet, Venus occults the line of sight: a blackout of up to \SI{65}{h}, during which about \SI{14508}{km} are flown with no contact. This work estimates the trajectory of a Venus aerobot across the occlusion by fusing an IMU, a Sun sensor, a radar altimeter and a barometer. A Multiplicative Extended Kalman Filter tracks the attitude and a particle filter tracks the position, coupled in a cascade that re-injects every transferred uncertainty as an inflated measurement covariance. Monte Carlo campaigns of 100 runs give a position RMSE of \SI{26.7}{km}, never worse than \SI{34.3}{km}, within the \SI{50}{km} requirement. A single Sun channel raises convergence from about 60\% to 98--99\%, while the accuracy is set by the onboard atmospheric model rather than by the Sun sensor.
\end{abstract}
